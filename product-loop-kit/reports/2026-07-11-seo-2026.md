## 2026 SEO findings (what changed / what matters now)
1. **Thin content is a dataset problem, not a template problem.** If you can strip the modifier word (the token symbol) and the remaining page is generic, the dataset is too shallow. Google 2026 detects and skips low-value programmatic pages. → Our new $100K/≥1-pool token pages (014) carry real per-token pool data, but a single-pool page is thin. Mitigation = content depth + internal linking, not fewer pages.
2. **Indexing = inventory management.** A page meant to drive demand must be (a) indexable, (b) correctly canonicalized, (c) internally linked. Orphan pages (no internal links, not in a sitemap) rarely get indexed no matter how good. → 014 pages are currently orphaned; phase 2 must wire sitemap + internal links + resolve the canonical between `/tokens/<slug>` and `?token=`.
3. **IndexNow** (Bing/Yandex; Google still not official) instantly notifies engines of new/changed URLs — cheap win for freshly generated token pages.
4. **Index Coverage report is the source of truth** for what's indexed/excluded/stuck. Our 011/012/013 fixes are on 2-6 week GSC validation cycles — we should be reading the coverage classes over time, not guessing.

## Strategy note (resolves an apparent contradiction)
013 *tightened* the sitemap to stop advertising thin `?token=` shells; the human now wants newer-token traffic captured. These aren't in conflict: capture newer tokens via **014's rich static `/tokens/<slug>` pages** (real content), and keep the `?token=` shell gate strict. The static page becomes the indexable surface; the shell stays app-only. That's the canonical-consolidation call in 021.
