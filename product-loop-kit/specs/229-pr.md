# 229 — Spotlight packs v2, targeting leg: the ranker stops optimising for the biggest number

**Risk tier: HIGH** (builder + operator guess; verifier assigns independently). No product render path, no
router, no sitemap, no deps, no config — but this regenerates the artifact the human posts publicly under
their own name, and adds gates adjacent to the trust rails. Item 069, same file and same artifact, was
tiered HIGH for exactly this reason.

## Goal

You told us (Q6a, 2026-08-04) that the spotlight packs were blocked on two things: **better targets** and
**better visuals**. The visuals half is gated behind the 225 design reset, which you rejected — so it stays
parked, and nothing in this change touches how a card looks. This is the targets half.

## The intuition

`generate-spotlight.js` picks which pool to spotlight. Its selector was one line:

```js
.sort((a, b) => poolTotalApy(b) - poolTotalApy(a));   // highest APY wins
```

Sorting by headline APY sounds harmless. It isn't, because "highest APY" is a near-perfect proxy for
"riskiest, most volatile, most likely to be a temporary spike". So the list it produced was the opposite of
the product's own positioning on every axis that matters.

Measured against live DefiLlama data this session (15,679 pools, 2026-08-06):

| | what the old ranker did |
|---|---|
| candidates clearing the trust rails | **405** — `rwa` 386 · `stable` 10 · `degen` 9 |
| personas in its top 10 | **9 of 10 `degen`** — literally the entire 9-pool degen population, 2.2% of candidates, filling 90% of your shortlist. Exactly **one** `rwa` pool appeared, at rank 10 |
| protocol diversity in the top 10 | **6 distinct protocols** out of 173 available; `uniswap-v3` alone held **5 of the 10 slots** |
| its **#1 recommendation** | `concrete · SRROYUSDC` — **86.51% APY, against that pool's own 30-day mean of 4.51%.** A 19× spike |

That last row is the one that matters most. The ranker's top suggestion was a number that is not what the
pool pays — it's what the pool paid for a moment. Post that, and the rate is gone before anyone clicks.
CLAUDE.md's rule is "honest numbers beat exciting numbers"; the selector was doing the exact opposite,
and nothing in the pipeline was looking at `apyMean30d` at all.

## What changed, and why

### 1. Two hard gates — refuse, never demote

Both are layered **on top of** the existing rails (`APY_SANITY_LIMIT`, the $10M TVL floor, the Curve
ceiling). Nothing was relaxed, renamed, or re-valued.

- **`isRepresentativeRate`** — a pool's headline must be within 50% of its own `apyMean30d` (or within
  0.5 percentage points, so a genuinely flat near-zero pool isn't failed by a division artifact). If
  `apyMean30d` is missing, the answer is **no**: absence of evidence of representativeness is not evidence
  of representativeness, and your name is on the post.
- **`isFundableForever`** — the pack's entire premise is a forever number, so a pool whose own effective
  rate can't produce one can't be a target.

Both are enforced in `pickPool`'s explicit `--pool <id>` path too, and the error message **names the gate
that failed** — the same refuse-don't-demote contract the rails already had.

### 2. A story score, replacing the sort key

Four signals, each in `[0,1]`, computed over the gated candidate set **derived at call time** — never a
baked-in table, so it self-corrects as live data moves:

| signal | what it measures | why it's here |
|---|---|---|
| `smallProtocol` | percentile of the protocol's aggregate railed TVL, inverted | your 2026-07-12 rule was "Curve is the upper bound, **aim lower in general**" — the old ceiling was a yes/no; this grades it |
| `unusualRate` | percentile of the pool's APY **within its own persona band** | this is the subtle one. Band-relative means a 9% stablecoin pool reads as unusual and a 25% degen pool reads as ordinary. It is **not** a rename of "highest APY" — that distinction is what stops the old behaviour creeping back in |
| `freshness` | percentile of `count` (days tracked), inverted | your "fresh listings with ≥$10M TVL" — the TVL floor is already a hard gate, so freshness can never let a thin pool through |
| `rateRepresentative` | how far *inside* the honesty gate the pool sits | rewards being comfortably honest, not merely barely-passing. Absolute, not a percentile — it's a property, not a preference |

Three of the four are **percentile ranks**, deliberately: a percentile has no tunable constant in it, so
there is no dial anyone had to guess at.

**The weights are equal, and that is a deliberate refusal rather than laziness.** Zero spotlight packs have
ever been posted, so there is no outcome data to fit weights against. Any asymmetry would be a claim
stronger than the evidence supports (RAZOR.md). The moment posted packs produce outcomes, this is the first
thing worth revisiting — it's flagged in the code comment.

### 3. A derived one-line hook

The angle is whichever of **`smallProtocol` / `unusualRate` / `freshness`** scores highest —
`rateRepresentative` is deliberately excluded from being an angle, because "the rate is what it's been"
is a precondition, not a story. (Measured: including it collapsed 229 of 369 candidates onto one template;
excluding it gives a 130/128/111 split.)

Three templates, one per angle. Every figure in them comes from the pack's own already-railed fields, the
forever clause always quotes the **haircut-applied** rate (never the headline), and it's dropped entirely
rather than fabricated when there's no forever number. Two wording rules are enforced by test: no
`save up` / `afford` / `budget` (CLAUDE.md's ban-list), and **"has been tracked N days", never "is N days
old"** — `count` is DefiLlama's tracking window, not the pool's real age, and the pack must not claim the
stronger fact.

### 4. Cadence: one protocol, once

`buildCadence`'s next-up list now takes at most one pool per protocol **and** skips protocols you've already
been given a pack for. That second half was a real bug caught in review, not by a test — see below.

the restyle is the visuals leg and stays gated.

## How this was checked

- `node run-tests.js --lane=plain` → **52/52, fail=0**. No pre-existing failures on this branch or on main.
- `test_spotlight.js` 60/60, `test_spotlight_packs.js` 11/11.
- **Non-vacuity, four separate mutations** — each rule disabled *alone*, so "four working rules" is
  distinguishable from "one working rule and three dead ones". Each went RED, then restored byte-identical
  (md5 verified): `isRepresentativeRate`, `isFundableForever`, the story-score sort, the cadence dedupe.
  Two of the four were re-run independently by the operator, reaching the same baseline md5.
- Tests assert **invariants over a population derived at test time**, not hardcoded pools. `concrete ·
  SRROYUSDC` appears only as a **positive control** for the representativeness gate — it is never the
  definition of the check, and the constants are declared independently of it.

## Deviations from the spec

All in `229-notes.md`; the two worth your attention:

1. **`rateRepresentative`'s normalisation** was the one place the spec's shorthand had to be interpreted.
   It's normalised so that "passes the gate" and "scores well on the term" use one shared function, rather
   than two formulas that could drift apart.
2. **The cadence covered-protocol bug was found in review, not by a test.** The first implementation
   deduped only *within* the next-up list, so `liminal-basis` was simultaneously a shipped pack and the #1
   next candidate — your very next post would have re-tagged the protocol you'd just posted. The tests as
   written could not have caught it (they only ever examined the next-list in isolation), which is why the
   fix ships with a new assertion that compares the next-list against the covered set. Recorded as a
   process finding, not papered over.

## The honest limits

- **This does not close its class, and the item says so with a number.** The predicate proved out here — *a
  headline figure may not be surfaced as a recommendation unless it's checked against its own series' recent
  mean* — is enforced in the spotlight ranker and **nowhere else**. `generate-token-pages.js`,
  `generate-chain-pages.js` and the analytics grid's sort modes all still rank on current APY with no such
  check. That's **1 of 4** ranked surfaces. Of the 405 rail-clearing pools, **36 (8.9%)** carry a headline
  more than 50% from their own 30-day mean; they're excluded from spotlights now, but nothing stops them
  ranking on the SEO surfaces, which is where the traffic actually lands. Filed as **backlog 242**.
- **Whether you post is the real gate, and no code can open it.** This is a build-leg ship: three honest,
  story-ranked, hook-carrying packs now exist. If nothing is posted within 60 days, the honest next question
  is whether X is the right channel at all — *not* whether to re-tune the ranker.
- The 405→369→289 funnel numbers are from one live fetch on 2026-08-06. Rates drift daily; the committed
  packs' tests assert self-consistency against each pack's own recorded numbers, never against live rates.
