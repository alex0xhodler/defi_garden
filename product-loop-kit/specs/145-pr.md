# 145 — PR explainer (HIGH tier)

**One line:** a single glitched day in a pool's rate history was silently producing a
respectable-looking "rate stability" score that ranked that pool ahead of honest ones — now any
KPI derived from an out-of-rail rate point is omitted instead of computed.

## Goal

Stop the product from deriving a trust signal out of data the product itself declares not credible.

## The intuition

DeFi Garden has one hard trust rail on rates: `APY_SANITY_LIMIT = 1000`. A pool advertising more
than 1000% APY is not credible, so the app demotes it, flags it ⚠, forces High risk, and never lets
it into a plan. That rail is enforced **at display time, on `totalApy`**.

Since item 087 we also keep a 30-day rolling *history* of each pool's rate (`data/history/*.json`)
and derive KPIs from it: mean, standard deviation, momentum, TVL trend, and the 117 "rate-stability
Sharpe" — `(mean rate − 4% risk-free) / volatility`. That Sharpe is not decorative: 117.2 ships it
as the analytics **"Risk-adjusted" sort**, and 117.3 uses it as the planner's near-tie-break when
picking which pool a saver's garden goes into.

Nothing applied the rail to the *history series*. So one bad day poisons everything downstream:

```
pool 201e5f6e-…  balancer-v2 WSTETH-AAVE, Ethereum, TVL $12.3M, current APY 0.26%

07-14 0.8107 | 07-15 0.5554 | 07-16 0.4765 | 07-17 0.4027 | 07-18 0.36 | 07-19 0.1497
07-20 260768.6404   ← 260× the rail. A DefiLlama data glitch.
07-21 0.5983 | 07-22 1.2518 | 07-23 0.3728 | 07-24 0.2506 | 07-25 0.2622
```

That one point drags `apyMean` to **21,731** and `apyStdev` to **72,072** — and then the *ratio* of
those two garbage numbers lands at a completely innocuous-looking **`apySharpe: 0.3`**.

That is what makes this bug class nasty, and it is the same shape as items 122 and 144: the guards
we already had were all magnitude guards. 122 capped `|Sharpe| ≤ 50`. 144 bounded `apyMean30d` by
the rail. Both would wave `0.3` straight through, because `0.3` is not a big number — it is a small
number computed from big garbage. The `audit-app.js` scanner (item 142) ran clean over 10 rendered
surfaces this tick, 0 findings, for exactly that reason (`ABSURD_MAGNITUDE = 1e11`).

The consequence in the product: in the "Risk-adjusted" sort, pools with *no* score sort last (honest
— "not enough history yet"). This pool carried a *numeric* 0.3, so it sorted **above every honest
null-Sharpe pool**, on the strength of a number that means nothing. And in the planner, when two
candidate pools are within `eps` on APY, the higher Sharpe wins the tie-break — a garbage 0.3 could
beat a genuinely steadier pool and land in a cautious saver's garden.

## What changed and why

The house precedent from 122 and 144 is **honest omission, never a clamped or cleaned value**. So:
if a rate point is out of rail, we do not clamp it, we do not drop it and recompute around it — we
decline to publish the KPIs that read it.

The rule is per-KPI, and deliberately precise about *which points each KPI actually reads*, so we
never null more than the evidence demands:

| KPI | Reads | Rule |
|-----|-------|------|
| `apyMean`, `apyStdev`, `apySharpe` | the whole series | `null` if **any** point > rail |
| `apyMomentum` | first and last point only | `null` if **either endpoint** > rail |
| `tvlTrend` | TVL only | unchanged — rate-independent |
| `historyPoints`, `firstSeen` | tracking facts | unchanged — we did track those days |

The real pool is the case that justifies the split: its glitch is in the *middle*, its endpoints
(0.8107 → 0.2622) are both fine, so `apyMomentum: -0.56` survives and stays truthful.

### Diff in reading order

**1. `compute-kpis.js` — the rail mirror (+6 lines).** A local `APY_SANITY_LIMIT = 1000` next to the
existing `SHARPE_*` constants, commented with `app.js:800` as the source of truth. The file's header
contract previously read "NEVER reads or relaxes the trust rails"; it now reads "never *relaxes*",
with an explicit note that it mirrors the rail read-only to bound its **own** derived math. That
distinction is the whole item: the rail stays owned upstream, and this script only ever *adds* a
bound.

**2. `compute-kpis.js` — `computeKpis()` (+8/−4).** Two booleans and four guarded fields:

```js
const seriesRailed   = apys.some(a => a > APY_SANITY_LIMIT);
const momentumRailed = first.apyTotal > APY_SANITY_LIMIT || last.apyTotal > APY_SANITY_LIMIT;
…
apyMomentum: (enough && !momentumRailed) ? round(last.apyTotal - first.apyTotal, 2) : null,
apyStdev:    (enough && !seriesRailed)   ? round(sd, 2) : null,
apyMean:     seriesRailed ? null : round(mean, 2),
apySharpe:   seriesRailed ? null : apySharpe,
```

Every pre-existing guard (`SHARPE_MIN_POINTS` 8, `SHARPE_MIN_STDEV` 0.05, `SHARPE_ABS_MAX` 50, the
`enough` ≥2-points gate, the `tvlTrend` divide-by-zero guard) is untouched and still runs first.
`apyMean` is the one field that had no `null` path before — it gains one, which is why criterion 6
of the verification brief was "check every reader of `kpis.apyMean` for null-safety".

**3. Data (`data/pools-snapshot.json` + the two enriched slices).** Regenerated for the affected
pool so the fix is live on merge rather than on the next CI bake:

```
before: {…, apyMomentum:-0.56, apyStdev:72072.51, apyMean:21731.18, apySharpe:0.3,  tvlTrend:-0.0954}
after:  {…, apyMomentum:-0.56, apyStdev:null,     apyMean:null,     apySharpe:null, tvlTrend:-0.0954}
```

**4. Tests.** `test_compute_kpis.js` +75 lines of unit assertions (whole-series null; poisoned-middle
keeps momentum, using the real 12-point history; poisoned endpoint nulls momentum; an all-sane series
stays byte-identical, including the exactly-at-the-rail edge). New `test_kpi_rail_history.js` (308
lines) is the rendered acceptance in real Chromium against the real committed snapshot: the affected
pool sits with its null-Sharpe peers in the Risk-adjusted sort, and its pool-detail page renders the
neutral "tracking this pool's rate for 12 days" note with no steadiness claim, no `NaN`/`undefined`/
`null` text, zero page errors. Wired into the `npm test` chain.

## Deviations from the spec

**One, and it is the reviewable risk in this PR.** The spec said to regenerate the data by running
`node compute-kpis.js`. A literal full run rewrites 428 files and drifts ~1,596 *other* pools' KPIs —
drift that reproduces identically with the **unmodified** script (proven by a `git stash` baseline),
i.e. it is pre-existing snapshot-vs-history skew, not caused by item 145. Shipping it would have
buried a 1-pool fix inside an unrelated 428-file diff.

So the builder isolated the code-only delta (fixed-regen vs unfixed-regen over identical input: zero
churn outside the target pool) and applied only that on top of clean `HEAD`. Verified independently:
exactly **1** pool's `kpis` object churned across all three data files, **0** non-KPI fields churned,
and the only other delta is the snapshot's `generatedAt` timestamp.

The honest caveat: `data/pools-snapshot.json` is a generated artifact, and this PR edits it without a
full regen — which cuts against CLAUDE.md's "regenerate, don't hand-edit" rule. The mitigations are
that the written value is exactly what the fixed generator produces for that pool from the committed
history (not an invented number), and that `sitemap-update.yml` runs `compute-kpis.js` on the next
bake and regenerates the whole set anyway. The pre-existing 1,596-pool drift is left as-is and is
worth its own ticket.

## Considered and rejected

*Excluding* out-of-rail points and computing from the survivors. Here that yields 11 clean points and
a real Sharpe of about −12.7, which is genuinely more informative. Rejected because it presents a
score as though we had a clean history, and it forces a semantic change on `historyPoints`, which
three pool-detail render gates and the "tracking for N days" copy read. Nulling invents nothing and
ripples nowhere. Recorded for the human — revisit only on an explicit call.

## Not built (follow-ups)

- The upstream fix: stop the poller/snapshot from *recording* an out-of-rail point at all.
- Extending anomaly **flagging** to `apyMean30d` (144-notes' second follow-up) — changes rendered risk
  app-wide.
- Teaching `audit-app.js` a rail-relative percentage check so the scanner can see this class itself.
- The pre-existing 1,596-pool snapshot-vs-history KPI drift.

2. Why does `apyMomentum` get a *different* rule from `apyStdev`, and which real-world fact about
   pool `201e5f6e-…` makes that distinction matter rather than being pedantry?
3. The header comment changed from "never reads or relaxes the trust rails" to "never relaxes". What
   would have been misleading about leaving it as-is, and why is the new wording still true?
4. Why was excluding the glitch day and computing a Sharpe from the remaining 11 points rejected,
   even though it produces a more informative number?
