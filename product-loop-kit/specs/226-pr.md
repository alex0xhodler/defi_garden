# 226 — PR explainer (HIGH tier: full walkthrough + quiz)

## Goal

Google has fetched most of this site and declined it. The GSC screenshots the human shared on 2026-08-04:
**3,274 indexed vs 38,124 known-not-indexed**, of which `Crawled — currently not indexed` is **18,555**
and `Soft 404` is **3,246**. In 60 days of Mixpanel there are **zero** google.com referrals — while
bing+ddg+ecosia+yandex sent ~14 and perplexity.ai sent 2, unprompted. So the estate is not failing to be
*found*; it is being found and judged not worth indexing.

Q3b of the 2026-08-04 strategy interview decided the response: **split the audiences.** Everything stays
live for agents and for the IndexNow engines that actually send traffic. Google's *sitemap view* shrinks
to a small, demand-plausible head. Nothing is deleted.

## The intuition

A sitemap is not a list of pages that exist. It is a claim: *these are the pages worth your crawl budget.*
Submitting 4,851 URLs on a domain with no authority makes that claim about 4,851 pages, and Google
answered by declining 85% of them and ranking none of the rest. The strongest de-prioritisation signal
available without deleting anything is to stop making the claim about the tail.

The second idea is that the repo already contained the right threshold and had been applying it in only
one place. `generate-sitemap.js` gates its app-view URLs at **≥2 pools clearing $10M TVL and the anomaly
rail** (item 013). `generate-token-pages.js` mints a *page* at **$100K / ≥1 pool / no cap**. That 20× gap
IS the scaled-thin-content mass. Head-selection therefore invents no new quality idea — it applies the
gate that already existed to the sitemaps that never had it.

## What changed, in reading order

**1. `generate-sitemap.js` — the head predicate (single source of truth).**
`HEAD_MIN_RAILED_POOLS = SITEMAP_MIN_QUALIFYING_POOLS` — bound by reference, not re-typed, so the two
gates cannot drift. `railedTokenPoolCounts(pools)` / `railedChainPoolCounts(pools)` count pools passing the
existing `isQualifyingPool` rail; `isHeadToken`/`isHeadChain` are the only place the comparison happens;
`selectHeadTokens`/`selectHeadChains` return the sets the generators filter through. Mirror rule applied
in the same commit: `generateSitemapSuite`'s own inline per-token qualifying loop was **deleted** and
replaced by a call to `railedTokenPoolCounts` — there is now one implementation, not two that could drift.

**2. `generate-sitemap.js` — `EMIT_APP_VIEW_SITEMAPS = false`.** The three app-view families
(`sitemap-chain-<Chain>.xml` ×103, `sitemap-category-<Cat>.xml` ×6, `sitemap-tokens-all.xml`) are still
*computed* — their honest "N dropped below quality gate" logs still print — but never written or indexed.
The pre-existing `cleanupStaleSitemaps()` (080) then removes the 108 previously-written files, because
they are absent from `writtenFilenames`. That is an artifact deletion; the URLs themselves stay live,
self-canonical (human directive 2026-07-11) and linked from every static page's "view in app" CTA, so
nothing is orphaned. One honest log line reports the suppressed count (438 today), computed not hardcoded.

**3. `generate-token-pages.js` / `generate-chain-pages.js` — the filter is on the SITEMAP only.**
Pages are still written for every ranked token and chain (2,085 and 87 today). Only the list handed to
`renderTokenSitemap`/`renderChainSitemap` is filtered through the head predicate. Hub and A–Z URLs stay
in full. Both log both numbers, e.g. `102 head URLs of 2085 generated pages, + 28 hub/A–Z URLs` — so a
future reader can never mistake "left the sitemap" for "stopped existing".

**4. `generate-token-pages.js` — `assertNonEmptyPages()`**, called by both generators right after ranking.
Throws, naming every offending slug, if any record's displayed pool list is empty. Today it fires on
nothing: `rankTopTokens`/`rankTopChains` already admit only records with ≥1 railed pool (and chains
additionally need a visible non-zero yield, 030/032/033). The value is that the invariant is now enforced
at generation instead of held by convention.

**5. `indexnow-ping.js` AND `generate-llms.js` — the inversion trap, closed in both places.**
The second one was missed on attempt 1 and caught by the verifier; it is the more important of the two.
`generate-llms.js` derives its entire URL population by parsing `sitemap.xml` and its children, so the
daily CI cron (`sitemap-update.yml:111-124` runs `generate-sitemap.js` then `generate-llms.js`
back-to-back) would have collapsed `llms.txt`/`llms-full.txt` from 4,851 URLs / 201KB to **329 URLs /
21KB** — shrinking the *agent* surface to Google's head. Q3b authorises de-listing from **Google's
sitemap view** and explicitly requires agents keep everything, so that would have been an unauthorised
de-indexing of SEO surface (NEVER list) and a direct hit on the north star's own leg (A), agent
consumption. Both generators now derive their full-estate population from the served estate on disk via
the same single `collectEstateUrls()` — one implementation, not two.

`indexnow-ping.js` was the half caught during the build. Its submission list used to be parsed out of
`sitemap-token-pages.xml`. Shrinking that sitemap would have silently shrunk **Bing and Yandex** to the
head too — precisely inverting Q3b, which keeps the full estate for the engines that actually send us
traffic. The new `collectEstateUrls(rootDir)` scans the directories the generators write
(`tokens/`, `ko/tokens/`, `tokens/az/`, `ko/tokens/az/`, `chains/`, `ko/chains/`) and maps each `*.html`
to its live URL. Deriving from the served estate rather than from a sitemap means the two cannot drift.

**6. Tests.** Four new: `test_sitemap_head_curation.js` (18 assertions), `test_indexnow_full_estate.js`
(11), `test_soft404_empty_pages.js` (8), and `test_llms_full_estate.js` (attempt 2, asserting the llms
population is a strict superset of the head, contains the count-1 tail token SKY, and clears a floor the
head alone could never reach) — all drawing their population from real generated output, none from a
hand-written fixture. Registered in `package.json`'s `test:serial`, which `run-tests.js` parses as the
single source of truth and `test_test_registry.js` enforces. Three existing tests were updated where they
encoded the pre-226 sitemap contract (`test_sitemap_xml.js`, `test_sitemap_category_urls.js`,
`test_vercelignore.js`), each commented in place naming this item.

**Non-vacuity, proven in-session, each rule neutered separately** (so "two working rules" is
distinguishable from "one working rule and one dead one"):
- mutant (i) `HEAD_MIN_RAILED_POOLS = 0` → head count 697 > 500 → assertion RED.
- mutant (ii) `EMIT_APP_VIEW_SITEMAPS = true` → head count 767 > 500 → assertion RED.
- `generate-sitemap.js` byte-identical before and after both mutants. (The test computes and compares
  the hash at runtime; it does not assert a literal. An earlier draft of this file quoted
  `6f0f5b22…`, the hash as of the builder's first run — the operator-review tightening described above
  changed the file afterwards, so that literal was stale by the time it shipped. Current committed
  hash: `096a620ebe3dcf09d4a6aaa40616f15d`. Caught by the verifier on attempt 2.)

## The measurement (controlled, per `playbooks/seo-surface-regen-delta.md`)

One shared fixture; baseline regenerated in an isolated `origin/main` worktree; effect read off the two
`<loc>` sets, never off `git diff`.

**4,851 → 329. ADDED: 0.** Dropped 4,522, entirely within the intended class: 337 `?token=`/`?poolTypes=`,
101 `?chain=<chain>`, 3,966 tail `/tokens/<slug>` (en+ko), 118 tail `/chains/<slug>` (en+ko). Zero
unexplained. All 12 spot-checked majors (usdc, usdt, eth, weth, wbtc, dai, sol, usde, steth, wsteth,
susds, cbbtc) are IN the head. Every head URL has a real file on disk. 1,992 de-listed token pages
verified still served, still carrying their `.md` twins. `robots.txt` byte-unchanged.

The cut is not free, and the honest version is not "the rule is not greedy": it drops recognisable
names. **SKY** (~$973M aggregate TVL, 1 pool ≥$10M), and chains **Aptos** (~$849M), **Bitcoin**,
**Linea**, **TON** — all on the same one-railed-pool shortfall. Inherited from item 013's existing
threshold rather than newly invented, and defensible (the gate asks whether an entity has ≥2 pools a
cautious saver could actually use), but it is a real cost and the first knob to revisit if the human's
GSC read says the head is too thin.

## Deviations from spec

Seven, all in `226-notes.md`. The two that matter: the head predicate lives inside `generate-sitemap.js`
rather than a new `head-selection.js` (a separate module would create a require cycle on a file that
already does require-time extraction from `PoolDetail.js`); and the builder's `.github/workflows/` edit
was **reverted** after the builder discovered my territory note claiming CI never stages
`sitemap-main.xml` was simply wrong — `git add -A -- 'sitemap*.xml'` on the next line already stages it
and the 108 deletions. HIGH-risk config stays untouched.

## What this does not claim

It does not claim to have eliminated GSC's 3,246 soft-404s. The whole-estate scan found **0** pages with
an empty main table, so the soft-404 predicate is now enforced but was already structurally satisfied;
GSC's soft-404 population is not readable from inside the loop, and the "it's the client-rendered filter
views" story is a hypothesis, labelled as one. It does not claim Google will respond — the spec itself
rates that leg a *guess*. And it leaves a class open with a number: **130 head pages now carry the entire
Google bet, and 0 of them have received the depth work** that would make them worth ranking. That is
follow-up work, unticketed as of this ship.

## Quiz

1. The item applies a "≥2 railed pools" gate to the static-page sitemaps. Where did that threshold come
   from, and why is it written as `HEAD_MIN_RAILED_POOLS = SITEMAP_MIN_QUALIFYING_POOLS` rather than `= 2`?
2. 4,522 URLs left the sitemaps. How many pages were deleted, and what is the mechanism that keeps the
   de-listed pages reachable by crawlers that no longer see them in a sitemap?
3. `indexnow-ping.js` was changed even though IndexNow has nothing to do with Google. What would have
   broken if it had been left alone, and why is scanning directories a stronger fix than pointing it at a
   different sitemap file?
4. The non-vacuity proof neuters two rules *separately* rather than mutating once. What failure mode does
   the separate treatment detect that a single mutation would miss?
5. The notes say the soft-404 gate fires on nothing today. Why was it added, and what does that imply
   about the claim "this item killed the 3,246 soft 404s"?
