# 208 — widen the kpi/history population: the measurement, and why it says stop · HIGH

## Goal

Item 208 was filed as **measure-then-decide, not build-now**. Its deliverable is a number and a verdict,
not a feature. The question: 88.5% of our `?pool=` SEO deep links land on pools the product has no rate
history for, so the three trust notes on the north-star page render nothing for them (item 207 shipped the
honest disclosure this morning; 208 is the structural half — *should we go get the data?*).

The spec pre-authorized both outcomes and said so in bold: **"Stopping at 207 is a successful outcome, not
a failure."** This PR is that stop, with the receipts.

## Intuition

Three floors exist in this product and they are not the same number, which is the whole reason this item
is hard:

| floor | who applies it | why |
|---|---|---|
| **$100K** | the SEO estate (`generate-token-pages.js`) | human directive 2026-07-11 — deep-link the long tail |
| **$10M** | `data/pools-snapshot.json` (059) | front-end **payload** size |
| **$10M** | `src/poller-core.js:48` → D1 | mirrors the snapshot's rail at write time |

KPIs are derived from retained history. History is retained at $10M. Deep links are minted at $100K. So the
gap is not a bug anyone introduced — it is two correct floors meeting. Closing it means retaining history
for ~5× more pools, every day, forever. 059 set the $10M floor *for payload reasons*, so re-widening
naively undoes the thing it exists to protect. Hence: price it first.

## What was measured, and what it cost

Everything below was generated for real against one cached live-feed fetch, using the repo's own
`slimPoint` and the committed history envelope. Full method, commands and drift disclosures:
`208-notes.md`.

**The history (the expensive half):**

| variant | ids | one point, raw | ×30 total | delta vs today's 1.221 MB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| today's committed point | 732 | 40,704 B | 1.221 MB | — |
| deep-linked only — *not implementable* | 3,670 | 203,038 B | 6.091 MB | 4.870 MB |
| **union** (deep-linked ∪ snapshot) | 3,979 | 219,610 B | **6.588 MB** | **5.367 MB** |
| **sub-rail-only sidecar** (additive) | 3,247 | 179,010 B | **5.370 MB** | **5.370 MB** |

**The delivery (the cheap half):** the lean `id → kpis` artifact, 182's envelope, fetched only on
pool-detail — **128,370 B gzipped** for all 3,670 ids, **113,381 B** for the sub-rail set. The 250 KB gate
passes with ~2× headroom.

**The question that could have collapsed everything:** does D1 already cover sub-rail pools? **No.**
`src/poller-core.js:48` re-applies the same $10M floor at write time — *"the ONLY drop"*. Only **423 of
3,670 (11.53%)** deep-linked pools clear $10M; median deep-linked TVL is **$516,638**.

## The decision

> *proceed only if the widened history costs **< 5 MB** total across the 30 retained points **and** the lean
> artifact is **< 250 KB gzipped** **and** the initial grid payload is provably unchanged.*

Criterion 1 fails. Criterion 2 passes. Criterion 3 is Phase-2-only and was never reached. **One failure is
enough — NO-GO, park with the measurements.**

The failure is robust, not marginal, and that is the part worth checking rather than taking on trust:

- fails on **every implementable variant** (6.588 MB / 5.370 MB),
- fails under **both unit conventions** (5.367 MB = 5.119 MiB; 5.370 MB = 5.122 MiB),
- fails under **both readings** of "costs … total" (total-at-retention *and* marginal delta),
- and the population it is sized against **grows daily** — the estate regenerates from the live feed at
  $100K every night, so thin headroom is not headroom.

**The one assumption, stated plainly rather than buried:** the history gate is read as **raw** bytes. The
spec asks for the artifact "gzipped" one item after asking for the history's "byte cost" / "repo-size
delta", so the distinction reads as deliberate. On gzipped bytes the history criterion *would* pass
(≈2.92 MB, ~2 MB of headroom) and the verdict would flip. If the human meant git-stored bytes, this NO-GO
is wrong and should be reversed — that is a one-sentence correction, and it is written into the notes,
this PR, and the BACKLOG row so it cannot be missed.

## Deviations from the spec

**D1 — measured three populations, not the one the spec names.** The spec frames the cost as "~3,669 pools
vs ~736". Sizing that literally would have measured a design that deletes a live feature: 309 of the 732
snapshot pools are not deep-linked, and their series backs the grid's shipped KPIs. The union and the
additive sidecar are the implementable floors, so those are what the verdict rests on. The deep-linked-only
number is reported too, labelled as not-implementable rather than quoted as "the cost".

**D2 — probed a backfill source the spec did not ask about.** One request (not a loop) to
`yields.llama.fi/chart/<id>` for a real $791,756 sub-rail pool: **HTTP 200, 1,190 points, 2023-03-01 →
today**. It does not change any cost above, and it does not change the verdict. It changes what we tell the
human: the blocker is **economic, not feasibility**. Worth the one request.

**D3 — re-measured the decisive numbers rather than accepting the build agent's.** Two `gzip -9`
invocations disagreed by ~4-5%. The operator's own `stat -c%s` / `gzip -9 -c | wc -c` figures are the ones
recorded, and they are the **more conservative** of the two. No verdict depended on the difference.

**Not a deviation but worth flagging: the item's risk tier.** The spec tiered 208 **HIGH** — correctly, for
the item *as filed*, since Phase 2 touches the data layer feeding every derived number, with a trust-rail
leak as its failure mode. The diff that actually ships here is **docs-only, ~zero product risk**. Both
readings are stated; the verifier assigns the tier independently, and the HIGH-tier ceremony (this
explainer + quiz) was written regardless, because the cheaper claim is the one that should have to argue
for itself.

## The trust rail, re-derived rather than assumed

The spec's hard invariant — *a sub-rail pool gaining a `kpis` object must never become eligible for the
grid, the planner or any ranking* — is **vacuously satisfied**: no pool gains a `kpis` object here, because
no code and no data file changed. `DEFAULT_MIN_TVL`, `APY_SANITY_LIMIT`, the anomaly flags and the degen
haircut are byte-untouched. Nothing is de-indexed. Nothing under `telegram-bot/`, `whatsapp-bot/` or
`workers/` is read, run or modified. No credentials, no money, no org-admin.

## Open for the human (both now have a price attached)

1. **The spec's own question:** is tracking ~3,670 sub-rail pools daily *desirable*, or is *"we track the
   larger pools daily"* a line worth keeping as positioning? The cost of the yes: ~5.4 MB of retained
   history churning daily, minimum.
2. **The shape the spec never considered:** compute sub-rail KPIs at CI time straight from
   `chart/<id>` and retain **only** the ~110 KiB-gzipped lean artifact — never widening `data/history/` at
   all, and backed by ~3.5 years of *real* history instead of accruing from zero. The trade moves to
   **~3,247 extra API requests per daily CI run** and a new external dependency. Deliberately not built:
   different architecture from the one specced, and `build.md` §1 forbids guessing an
   architecture-changing answer.
