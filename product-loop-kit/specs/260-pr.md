## Goal

numeral-collision instances and fixed **neither**. The ticket asked this item to reproduce both from
live data, choose a fix, and widen the guard to whatever the fix makes assertable.

## Intuition

A pool card puts a number and a name side by side in a fixed-width row. 246 told every numeral cell
"never wrap" — which fixed a real trust defect (a `⚠` glyph splitting from the number it qualifies)
and, for numbers too wide for their column, quietly replaced it with a different one: an
un-wrappable box has to go *somewhere*, so it grows sideways out of its column and prints on top of
its neighbour. The fix is to give the number's own container a **yield order**: the label next to it
is text and may shrink and ellipsize; the number never may, because a truncated number is a lie.

## What changed and why

**The ticket's stated hypothesis was wrong, and the measurement is what says so.** The BACKLOG row
proposed the fix was "a min-width/flex-shrink discipline on the byline or a truncation-with-title on
the project slug". Before writing any code, both instances were reproduced against the real rendered
UI and their boxes measured with `getBoundingClientRect()`:

| element (list view @768px, row grid `40px 246px 110px 130px 120px`) | left | right | width | clientW | scrollW |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `.pool-context-inline` (the byline) | 98.9 | **336.6** | 237.8 | 246 | 406 → clipped, ellipsis working |
| `.pool-apy-section` (its 110px track) | **348.2** | 454.6 | 106.3 | 110 | 110 |
| `.pool-apy-hero` | **311.5** | 454.6 | **143.1** | 148 | 148 |

The byline stops exactly at its own track edge (336.6 ≈ 348.2 − 12px gap). It is the **hero** that
escapes its 110px track by 36.7px leftward. So both of the ticket's proposed fixes aim at the party
that is already behaving. Instance (ii) is the same mechanism rotated: `.pool-tvl-section` is a
128.7px grid item whose content needs 148.3px (label 21 + gap 5.8 + value 121.5), and `.tvl-value`
(`nowrap`, `min-width: auto`) cannot shrink, so its box runs to right=222.3 — past the section edge
(202.7), into `.pool-cta-section` (left=210.7). An 11.6px overlap.

**Unified class:** *a `nowrap` numeral cell has no width discipline relative to its container, so
when its intrinsic width exceeds its allotted track, its box escapes into a neighbouring column.*
Note why the existing check D (`scrollWidth <= clientWidth`) is structurally blind to it: the
element's own box grows to fit its text, so nothing is clipped *inside* it — the box is oversized
relative to its **track**, which a self-overflow check cannot see.

## Why instance (i) is printed rather than asserted or fixed

Asserting it would make the suite permanently red for a defect this item is not authorised to fix;
burying it in a notes file is how a gap quietly becomes "covered". Printing it in the run's own
output is the honest middle: every run names the open collision and points at the spec.

It is not fixed because **four constraints are jointly unsatisfiable for an unbounded numeral
width**: (1) never truncate a numeral — trust rail; (2) never wrap a numeral — 246's shipped rule;
(3) list rows keep row-to-row alignment, and each `.pool-card` is its *own* grid with an identical
fixed template, so any content-driven track sizing misaligns the one anomalous row against every
other row and against the `.pool-columns` header; (4) no overlap. Unlike the TVL section, the APY
section holds no slack to yield — it stacks two numerals vertically and has no label to shrink. That
leaves exactly two fixes, and both exceed this item's authority:

- **(a)** make `.pools-list` one grid with `subgrid` rows so `max-content` resolves across all rows
  at once — a layout-system change to the **approved** list view, which standing decision 2026-08-05
  reserves for screenshot-first increments approved by the human one at a time;
- **(b)** bound how an above-rail APY is *displayed* — trust-rail-adjacent, and NORTH_STAR reserves
  anomaly-flag decisions to the human.

Recorded per RAZOR rather than guessed, and surfaced to the human in this run's report.

## A finding worth keeping: the first non-vacuity mutation was inert

The spec's non-vacuity criterion named a mutation — strip `flex-shrink: 1; min-width: 0` from
`.tvl-label` — and it **stayed green**. That is not a broken fix; it is a real property of flexbox
the builder root-caused by direct geometry measurement rather than papering over: a flex item's
automatic minimum size (`min-width: auto`) only contributes its content-based minimum while the
item's `overflow` is `visible`. Candidate A sets `overflow: hidden` on the label (needed for the
ellipsis at all), which already zeroes its effective minimum — so `min-width: 0` is redundant with
it, and `flex-shrink: 1` was flexbox's default all along.

~~The load-bearing declaration is therefore `overflow: hidden`.~~ **CORRECTED (verifier finding 3).**
That was too specific. The verifier removed *only* `overflow: hidden`, leaving `min-width: 0`, and
the scan stayed GREEN in all 8 combos. The accurate statement is one level up:

> The load-bearing condition is that **the label's effective minimum width be zero**. It is
> satisfied independently by `min-width: 0` **or** by `overflow: hidden`; either alone suffices, and
> only removing *both* turns the check red.

That is consistent with the builder's own 3-declaration mutation, which is the one that went RED —
naming `.tvl-value` vs `.calculate-yield-btn-new` at grid 1280/1540, both themes — followed by a
byte-identical restore.

**A verifier re-running the literal two-declaration mutation should expect green** — that confirms
this finding rather than contradicting the fix.

## Corrected class statement — with the right numbers

The first draft of this section said instance (ii) was "fixed and permanently guarded". **That was
an overstatement and the verifier falsified it.** What ships is not a width discipline; it is a
one-time purchase of **~19.6px — exactly two digits** — of headroom, bought by letting the label
collapse. Scaling the fixture pool brings the identical collision straight back, byte-identical on
this branch and on `origin/main`.

My first correction then made the *same* mistake one level down: it replaced "permanently guarded"
with a "~150px" bound inferred from the single counter-example I'd been handed. That was wrong by
2–3 digits. The real bound comes from a **threshold sweep** (7 magnitudes × {light, dark} ×
{1280, 1540}, two fixture shapes, `AnimatedNumber` settled before sampling):

| CSS | last CLEAN | first RED |
|---|---|---|
| shipped | `"$950000000.0B"` — **121.5px** (13 chars) | `"$9500000000.0B"` — **131.3px** (14 chars) |
| pre-260 | 101.9px | 111.7px |

Honest version: **instance (ii) is fixed up to ~122px of rendered numeral and reopens at ~131px.**
The fix moved the threshold by two digits; it did not remove it. Real `formatCurrency` output
abbreviates to ~55px, so every real TVL sits far below the bound. The shipped guard cannot see the
residual either, because its predicate is neighbour-intersection rather than containment — spelled
out, with the weaker predicate named, in spec 260.md's "Which predicate check E uses".

**Instance (i)** is not fixed at all (see above). Its numbers, re-derived from
`data/pools-snapshot.json` with the app's own predicate rather than copied forward: **18 of 7,340
pools (0.245%)** exceed `APY_SANITY_LIMIT` — the earlier "17 of 7,339 (0.23%)" was stale and is
corrected. Sane APYs top out at 75.6px inside a 110px track, so no non-anomalous pool can reach it.
The residual is smaller than first claimed, but state it in the right units: the governing quantity
is the **byline's right edge vs the hero's left edge**, and the byline is `<project> · <chain>`, not
the slug. The widest real anomalous byline is `kyberswap-fairflow · Robinhood Chain` (right edge
318, closest margin 29px), and **all 18 render clean at every viewport in both views** — so no real
pool satisfies both trigger conditions today. Note that a slug-length proxy would have got this
wrong: the longest slug is `aerodrome-slipstream` (20 chars) but the widest *byline* comes from an
18-char slug on a long chain name, so the two rankings disagree. The 57-char slug and the anomalous
APYs are each real; only their pairing is currently absent, and nothing prevents it.

**Retracted (verifier round 3):** a draft of this section also claimed all 18 anomalous pools sit
