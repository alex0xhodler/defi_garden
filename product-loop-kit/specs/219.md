# 219 — the audit has no OCCLUSION lens: leg (a), a universal occlusion signal on every audited surface

- **Backlog row**: 219, score 8.5, status READY at pickup, risk HIGH, **no spec file existed** — this spec was written by the build run of 2026-08-04 from documented in-repo evidence only: BACKLOG row 219 (the human's verbatim challenge of 2026-08-03), `specs/217.md` / `specs/217-notes.md`, `specs/218.md`, `playbooks/fixed-overlay-occlusion.md`, `playbooks/product-audit.md`, NORTH_STAR.md's 2026-07-10 autonomy trade and 2026-07-23 PRE-TRAFFIC MANDATE, and a direct read of `audit-app.js` on `main` @ `01ef8c1489`.
- **North star**: `pool_click{source ∈ {garden_cta, protocol_link}}` on pool-detail. Item 218 was the north-star emitter's own button, invisible and unclickable at first paint, and **every existing audit check scored that surface clean**. This item builds the check that would have failed.

## Problem

`audit-app.js` renders real pages in real Chromium, and every one of its checks is a **DOM read of a defect someone already thought to name**: `number-sanity`, `junk-slug`, `zero-yield-claim`, `empty-table`, `dead-end`, `dead-cta`, `degraded-cta`, `i18n`, `responsive`, `page-error`. `grep -n "screenshot" audit-app.js` returns **zero**. Nothing in the scanner asks the one question a human asks in half a second: *is this element actually visible, and can I actually press it?*

The cost is measured, not hypothetical:

| in-repo defect | what it was | what the audit reported |
|---|---|---|
| **217** (human-reported, iPhone) | fixed opaque `.app-footer` painted over the bottom band of pool-detail; hero prose sliced mid-sentence | 82 surfaces / **0 blocking findings**, pool-detail among them |
| **218** (found by 217's verifier, not by any audit) | the `garden_cta` anchor — the north-star metric's own emitter — fully behind the footer at 360×780, `elementFromPoint` returning `P.app-footer-hub-links` | same tick, clean |
| **179** | same class on bare `/`; fixed on one route and never ported to the others | never detected by the audit; found by hand |

NORTH_STAR.md's 2026-07-10 autonomy decision already priced this in — *"no pixel-level QA exists until 003 ships"*, hence the `needs human visual spot-check` stamp stapled to every render-path merge. This item **closes** that gap for the one defect class that is mechanically assertable, instead of continuing to pay it.

Both `checkResponsive` legs already in the file are near-misses that structurally cannot catch it: they measure the CTA's own box against the **viewport** (`box.x` inside `0..s.width`, non-zero area). An element with a perfect box, fully inside the viewport, with another element painted on top of it, passes both.

## Scope — leg (a) only, deliberately

BACKLOG row 219 specifies **two legs and says to ship the cheap one first**:

- **(a) occlusion signal — THIS ITEM.** Assert, on every audited surface, that no content element is covered by a `position: fixed`/`sticky` element, confirmed with `elementFromPoint` so it catches click *interception* and not just paint. Deterministic, assertable, no new dependency, no model in the loop.
- **(b) visual lens — NOT BUILT HERE.** Screenshot per surface/lens + an agent judging it, for the defect class no assertion can express (clipped pills, dead whitespace, misalignment — e.g. the `Risk Assessment: Low` pill overflowing its hero column in the human's 2026-08-03 screenshot). Leg (b) puts a non-deterministic judge inside a test-gated daily tick: it needs its own decisions about which model, what it costs per tick, how a screenshot verdict is made reproducible enough to gate on, and where the images live. That is a separate item with a separate risk call — see **Out of scope (filed, not silenced)** below. It is not started, not stubbed, and not half-landed here.

## Territory notes (read before writing code)

It branches on `s.kind` and **each branch returns early**: `loading` :3571, `static` :3595, `dead-pool` :3627, `grid` :3639, `landing` :3652, `planner` :3686, `bloom` :3741, and the `pool` fall-through ending :3928. There is no shared epilogue, so a universal check needs **one call site per branch** — the same call-site-duplication pattern `checkResponsive` already uses across five sites (and which `test_audit_768_lens.js` pins by count).
- `finding(surface, viewport, check, severity, detail)` :3514 is the only finding constructor. `blockingFindings()` :4523 treats **P0 and P1 as blocking**, P2 as advisory — so severity choice here decides whether the daily tick goes red.
- Pages are created at `{ width: s.width, height: 900 }` (:3527) — **always 900 tall**. This matters: item 218's CTA is buried at 360×**780** and completely clear at 360×900 (footer top moves from 722 to 842). *A naive occlusion check bolted onto the existing 900px pages would still not have caught 218.* Height is part of the defect.
- Existing occlusion measurement to reuse rather than reinvent: `test_footer_occlusion.js` (bottom-of-scroll geometry) and `test_cta_at_rest_occlusion.js` (at-rest geometry + `elementFromPoint` hit test, incl. its scroll-loop-with-assertion and its positive control). `playbooks/fixed-overlay-occlusion.md` steps 4–6 are the method; this item generalises them from two hand-written tests on one surface into a lens over all ~82.
- The overlay population in this design system (`grep -n "position: *\(fixed\|sticky\)"`): `.app-footer` (`style.css:2514`, opaque, `z-index:100`), `.google-header-sticky` (:904), `.theme-toggle` (:3914), `.modal-overlay` (:3587), `.global-filter-scrim` (:1246), `.gp-waitlist-backdrop` (`planner-styles.css:2442`), `.gp-bloom-checkout` (sticky, :489). The check must be written against this reality — a rule that flags a legitimately-open scrim, or content the user simply scrolled under a fixed header, would flood the tick and get the lens switched off.
- Helpers available for reuse: `pollFor` :3443, `waitForSelector` :3453, `makeErrorSink` :3501.

## Fix — `checkOcclusion(page, s, findings)`

A new function in `audit-app.js`, called once on the **success path** of all seven non-`loading` kind branches (the `loading` branch measures a mid-flight paint race and has no settled layout to judge; the three "did not render within Ns" early returns have no content to measure and keep their existing shape).

### Measurement geometry

Before measuring, resize to `{ width: s.width, height: OCCLUSION_HEIGHT }` with `OCCLUSION_HEIGHT = 780` — the exact height at which 218 was measured, and the shortest height in the documented evidence. Occlusion is height-dependent (218: buried at 780, clear at 900), so a lens that only ever looks at 900 is a lens with 218 in its blind spot. The call is last in each branch, immediately before the trailing `page-error` push, so the resize cannot perturb any other check.

Two passes:

1. **At rest.** `window.scrollTo({ top: 0, behavior: 'instant' })`, then **assert `scrollY === 0` before measuring** — `playbooks/fixed-overlay-occlusion.md`'s "computing a clear scroll target hides the at-rest bug" is precisely how 218 survived 217's build. Poll briefly for arrival (the file's own `pollFor`) rather than reading position once; keep the "skipped" **P2** for a genuinely stuck page.

   Smooth scrolling must be defeated **before this pass**, not just before pass 2 (see below): the `planner` driver clicks a `.gp-chip` (`audit-app.js:3717`) before the occlusion call, and a click can scroll the thread into view — so on a page that is not already at the top, an animated `scrollTo(0,0)` read one tick later degrades the at-rest leg (the leg that catches 218) into an advisory that measured nothing. One mutation at the top of the check covers both passes.

   A **short settle after the viewport resize** (~150ms) precedes measurement: the resize reflows and re-renders the React surfaces, and measuring inside that window produces both false and missed findings — the flood-then-get-switched-off failure mode.
2. **Bottom of scroll.** Scroll to `documentElement.scrollHeight`, loop until `scrollTop + innerHeight >= scrollHeight - 2` (max 8 attempts, short settle between, and a `scrollHeight` that changed since the previous attempt counts as "still settling", not as arrival). Skipped when the document does not scroll (`scrollHeight <= innerHeight` — the at-rest pass already covers that page). If the bottom is never reached, emit an advisory **P2** finding saying so, with the numbers — a pass that silently failed to scroll would be a vacuous green.

   **Smooth scrolling must be defeated first, or this whole leg is dead in production.** `style.css:2845` sets `html { scroll-behavior: smooth }`, so `window.scrollTo` *animates*: the first implementation of this check reported `bottom-of-scroll unreachable after 5 attempts … scrollTop=1663 … scrollHeight=2504` on the real `pool-detail-360` surface — 96.5% of the way there, i.e. an animation still in flight, misreported as an unreachable document. Set `scrollBehavior = 'auto'` on `documentElement`/`body` (a measurement-only mutation, applied after every other check has run) and pass `behavior: 'instant'`, then loop. Without this the 217/179 class — the exact class this lens exists to catch — is never actually measured on any page tall enough to have it.

### What counts as an overlay

Every element whose computed `position` is `fixed` or `sticky`, that is visible (non-zero rect, `checkVisibility({ visibilityProperty: true, opacityProperty: true })` where available), **excluding**:

- **Overlays covering ≥ 80% of the viewport area** — an open modal/scrim legitimately covers the page. Deliberate blind spot, documented: a full-screen scrim that should not be open is a different defect class (`dead-end`-shaped), not this one.

### What counts as a victim

In document order, capped at `OCCLUSION_CANDIDATE_CAP = 800` elements (if the cap truncates the scan, say so in an advisory **P2** finding on that pass — no silent caps):

- **interactive**: `a[href], button, input, select, textarea, [role="button"]`, or
- **text-bearing**: has a direct child text node of ≥ 3 trimmed characters,

that is visible, has a non-zero rect intersecting the viewport, is **not** itself positioned `fixed`/`sticky`, and is **not** contained in any collected overlay.

### The rule (two-position asymmetry — this is the load-bearing design decision)

- **At rest**, any collected overlay may occlude: first paint is what the user sees, and content hidden at first paint is a defect no matter where the overlay is anchored. This is 218's and 217's case.
- **At bottom of scroll**, only overlays **anchored to the bottom of the viewport** (`rect.bottom >= innerHeight - 2`) may occlude. Content covered by a *top*-anchored fixed header at max scroll is content the user can reveal by scrolling **up** — flagging it would put a false finding on every grid surface with `.google-header-sticky` and get the lens ignored. Content covered by a bottom-anchored overlay at max scroll can be revealed by **no** scroll position: that is exactly 217's and 179's class.

A victim is reported only when **both** hold:

1. **Geometry** — its rect intersects the overlay's rect, covering ≥ `OCCLUSION_MIN_COVERAGE = 0.25` of the victim's area for a **text-bearing** victim; **any** intersection qualifies for an **interactive** victim (a button whose lower half is buried is unpressable in practice — 218 was a 47px button in a 58px band).
2. **Hit test** — `document.elementFromPoint` at the victim's centre, and (interactive victims) at 75% of its height, returns the overlay or a descendant of it rather than the victim or a descendant of the victim. This is the check that distinguishes real burial from sub-pixel rect algebra, and it is what makes a *transparent* click-thief detectable: for interactive victims, opacity is irrelevant because interception is proven directly.

For text-bearing victims the overlay must additionally be **paint-opaque** (`background-color` alpha ≥ 0.5 or a non-`none` `backdrop-filter`) — a transparent overlay hides no prose, and claiming otherwise would be a false finding.

### Findings emitted

`check: 'occlusion'`, one finding per (pass × severity class), worst offender first (greatest covered fraction, ties broken by document order), with `+N more` when a pass has several — at most **4** blocking findings per surface, so a systemic layout bug cannot bury the rest of the tick's report:

- **P0** — an **interactive** victim is occluded. It maps 218 (the north-star emitter) to the same severity the backlog gave it, and it matches the file's existing convention that a dead/unreachable CTA is the worst thing a surface can do.
- **P1** — a **text-bearing** victim is occluded. 217's class (prose sliced by the footer).
- **P2** (advisory, non-blocking) — bottom-of-scroll unreachable, candidate scan truncated, or the check itself threw. The check must never crash a surface driver and must never fail silently.

Detail string carries: pass label (`at-rest (scrollY=0)` / `bottom-of-scroll`), viewport used, victim `<tag class="…">` + ≤80-char text snippet, victim rect, overlay `<tag class="…">` + rect, covered fraction, and which hit-point resolved to the overlay. A ticket must be actionable from the detail alone — `playbooks/product-audit.md`'s standard.

`checkOcclusion` is added to `module.exports` so the acceptance test can drive it as a function against a real page (the precedent `reconcilePrescanFindings` / `classifyCtaKind` / `ctaFindingSeverity` already set).

**Layer 1 — source-level (no browser; always runs, always able to fail):**

1. `checkOcclusion` is defined in `audit-app.js` and exported from `module.exports`.
2. It is called on the success path of **all seven** non-`loading` kind branches — assert exactly 7 `await checkOcclusion(` call sites, and assert the `loading` branch (from `if (s.kind === 'loading')` to its `return findings`) contains none.
3. `OCCLUSION_HEIGHT === 780` and the check name literal `'occlusion'` are present; the two-position asymmetry is pinned (a bottom-anchor gate exists for the bottom-of-scroll pass) so a later edit cannot quietly widen it into a header-noise generator.

**Layer 2 — real Chromium (only the browser launch itself may be skip-tolerant, per the 160 lesson; every assertion sits outside that catch):**

4. **No false positive on the north-star surface.** Real `home.html?pool=<STETH fixture id>` at 360×780, fixture-routed, minified sheets live: `checkOcclusion` returns **zero P0/P1 findings** — the product is healthy here today (217 + 218 shipped).
5. **RED PROOF on the real product — the criterion that makes this item worth shipping.** Same real page, with 218's fix mutated away in-page (`.app.pool-detail-view .app-footer { position: fixed; bottom: 0; left: 0; right: 0 }` + `padding-bottom: 0`, i.e. the exact pre-218 state): `checkOcclusion` reports **≥ 1 P0** finding whose detail names the `garden_cta` anchor (`href` containing `plan.html`). *This is the assertion that proves the lens would have caught 218, rather than asserting it in prose.*
6. **Bottom-of-scroll leg is not dead.** A fixture page (local, served by the test's own server or `setContent`) whose bottom content is clear at rest and covered by a bottom-anchored opaque fixed bar at max scroll yields a `bottom-of-scroll` finding; the same page with the bar `position: static` yields none.
7. **The lens does not flag ordinary layout.** A fixture page with a *top*-anchored fixed header, proper clearance, and content scrolled under it at max scroll yields **zero** blocking findings (the asymmetry rule, asserted, not assumed).
8. **The bottom-of-scroll leg is not vacuous ON THE REAL PRODUCT.** A real `runAudit({ only: ['pool-detail-360'] })` (a page whose `scrollHeight` ≈ 2504 at 360×780, i.e. genuinely scrollable) produces **no** `bottom-of-scroll unreachable` P2 — arrival is confirmed. This criterion exists because the first implementation failed it, silently, against `html { scroll-behavior: smooth }`.
9. **Integration.** `runAudit({ only: ['pool-detail-360'], … })` covers that surface, every finding is well-formed (`surface`/`viewport`/`check`/`severity ∈ {P0,P1,P2}`/`detail` all non-empty strings), and any `occlusion` finding this real run originates is **reported in `specs/219-notes.md`, not fixed here** (a detector's first real findings are the next tick's tickets — `playbooks/detector-signal-coverage.md`).

Every test run is timeboxed (5-minute foreground cap, 2026-07-11 standing decision); a run that cannot finish is documented in the notes, never waited on unbounded.

## The pre-existing red this lens exposes — `test_audit_app.js` (decided, not discovered late)

A working detector for a class with three known live instances will turn something red on its first run, and it did: `test_audit_app.js` case 1 asserts **ZERO P0/P1 across the app surfaces** (`grid-360` among them) and the lens reports real occlusion defects there. Handled per `playbooks/pre-existing-red-triage.md`, explicitly:

- **Classification — rule A, product regression, NOT a stale test and NOT the lens misfiring.** The assertion still describes what the product is supposed to do. The defects are in the product and predate this diff; the audit was green only because the class was *unmeasured*. So the burden of proof is on the *defect*, not the finding: it must be reproduced by direct measurement on the real page (a second, independent read outside `checkOcclusion`, resampled to rule out a timing race), and the finding must be quoted verbatim in `specs/219-notes.md`.
- **Never the two easy wrong moves**: do not weaken/scope-down `checkOcclusion` so the red disappears (that is laundering a true positive — the exact failure mode `detector-signal-coverage.md` exists to break), and do not delete case 1's zero-blocking measurement.
- **Split the gate** (the playbook's rule-E shape), following the precedent already inside case 1 — where real junk static pages "legitimately trip the new junk-slug check … a true positive, not a regression here" and the *scope* was narrowed rather than the check softened:
  1. case 1 keeps **ZERO P0/P1** for every check that is not `occlusion`, unchanged;
  2. for `occlusion`, it asserts findings occur **only** on the explicitly named, documented quarantined surface(s) — every other covered surface must be occlusion-clean, so a NEW occlusion defect anywhere else still turns it red;
  3. it additionally asserts **ZERO occlusion findings on every `pool-detail*` surface** — the 217/218 fixes' regression rail, now machine-watched;
  4. the quarantine block names each defect, cites `specs/219-notes.md`, and **prints a loud line when the quarantined surface comes back clean** so the quarantine is removed rather than inherited forever.
- **Fixing the grid/landing defects is a separate item.** This item ships the detector; its findings are tickets (reported in the notes, the LOG, and the run's report to the human).

## Risk

**HIGH** (NORTH_STAR.md risk policy, "when in doubt → HIGH"). No product render path changes and no trust rail is touched — `audit-app.js` is the auditor, `APY_SANITY_LIMIT`/`DEFAULT_MIN_TVL`/anomaly flags/degen haircut are not in the diff, no SEO surface is deleted, `package.json` gains a test step and **no dependency**, and nothing under `telegram-bot/`/`whatsapp-bot/`/`workers/` is read or written. It is HIGH because the diff exceeds the LOW 150-line cap and because it changes what the **daily tick blocks on**: a new P0/P1 class over ~82 surfaces can turn the tick red on its first run. That is the intended behaviour of a detector for a class with three known live instances — but it is a real change in the loop's own gating, so it gets the HIGH explainer treatment.

Two costs, accepted and stated rather than discovered later:

- **Wall clock.** Two `page.evaluate` passes + a resize + a short settle ≈ 0.3s per surface, ≈ 25s across a full 82-surface tick. The 192 rotation guard could therefore cover marginally fewer pool surfaces on a tick that is already near its budget. No screenshots, no extra page loads, no second navigation — the cost is bounded by construction.
- **First-run findings.** If the lens is right, its first real tick may surface defects on surfaces nobody has looked at (the `.theme-toggle`/`.gp-bloom-checkout` populations). Those are tickets, not regressions in this diff, and the per-surface cap of 4 keeps them from drowning the report.
