# Spec 192: 191 raised the rotation default to a ceiling nobody had measured — the render budget on the north-star surface is bounded by a constant, not by the clock

## Evidence
All of it already in the repo; nothing here is newly discovered by this run's own guesswork.

- **The ceiling, not the cost, is what limits coverage.** `audit-app.js:239-240`:
  `DEFAULT_POOL_SAMPLE = 6` and `MAX_POOL_SAMPLE = 6`. Item 191 raised the *default* 2 → 6 and stated

  bottleneck."* The 300s/5-minute foreground cap (standing decision 2026-07-11) has ~65% headroom

- **The gap 191 left open, in its own words.** `specs/191.md` §Change: *"If the timing measurement

- **What the remaining gap costs on the north-star surface.** `product-loop-kit/reports/2026-07-31.md`
  records 191 as *"DEFAULT_POOL_SAMPLE 2->6, full pass 370->124 ticks — a 3x constant factor, NOT

  pool-detail defect has a **~0.8%/day** chance of being rendered. Pool-detail has been the north-star
  surface since the 2026-07-23 standing decision, and the render-only classes the rotation exists to
  catch (`dead-cta`, i18n leaks, 360px clipping, console errors) are exactly what found items **136**,

  Marginal cost of a rendered pool-detail: **~0.19s** (+5s for 26 extra renders, 6 → 32) — the run is

    "11111111111111111111111111111111111111112" (|value| = 1.11e+40)* → **FALSE POSITIVE, and the more

    APY — an entirely ordinary pool). Its `underlyingTokens[0]` is
    `So11111111111111111111111111111111111111112` — the **wrapped-SOL mint address** — rendered as an

  **Neither is fixed by this item** (one-item rule); both are filed as their own backlog rows from this
  evidence. **The consequence for THIS item must be stated in the PR explainer and the log, not
  discovered later:** raising the default to 32 puts pool `3075a746` inside the ordinary rotation, so the

  precisely the 171 lineage (*"the product audit reports a blocking P0 for a defect its own rendered check
  did not confirm"*).

  What the pair actually proves is narrower and more honest than "the gap hides live P0s": at 6/tick the
  scanner had **not once rendered** either surface, so nobody could tell whether the classes it reports
  there are real. Widening turned two unknowns into two triaged answers inside one run.
- **The risk a bigger budget introduces is already named.** 191's decision rule marks the change
  REVERT-CANDIDATE *"if the run starts timing out or the loop begins skipping the audit to save time — a
  scanner that does not finish is strictly worse than one that covers 2 pools."* Today nothing enforces
  that: the sample size is a constant, and the run's wall-clock is whatever it turns out to be.

## Hypothesis
We believe **replacing the unmeasured ceiling with a measured one, and making the rotation leg
clock-aware, will multiply rendered coverage of the north-star surface again** (target: full pass well
under 124 ticks) **without ever putting the scanner's completion at risk**, because **191 measured the
per-render marginal cost as within noise while the 300s cap sits ~65% unspent.** Confidence:
**supported** for the throughput arithmetic (it is measured, not guessed); **unproven** for the thing
that would actually matter — whether widened rotation *originates* a P0/P1. At single-digit-%/day per
pool that stays unlikely inside one 14-day window; a null result there is uninformative, not negative,
and the explainer must say so.

This item buys **another constant factor, not closure.** Say so in the PR explainer. Full closure of the
render-only classes over 739 pools is an architectural question (parallel contexts / a persistent
browser), explicitly still not being answered here.

## Change
Three parts, all inside `audit-app.js` (loop tooling — no product surface).

**1. Set the ceiling from the measurement above** (the measuring is already done — do **not** re-run it;
the three timings in §Evidence are this run's operator measurements and are the justification of record):
- `DEFAULT_POOL_SAMPLE` **6 → 32** — the largest *measured* value, at 116s = 39% of the 300s cap, well
  inside the ≤180s (60%) budget this spec sets. Chosen because it was measured, not because it is round.
- `MAX_POOL_SAMPLE` **6 → 64** — deliberately **above** the default this time, so the env override can
  raise as well as lower and the next person is not blocked by the ceiling the way 191 was. 64 is bounded
  by the same measurement, not by taste: at ~0.19s/render, 64 extrapolates to ~122s, still under the
  180s budget. Update the constants' comments to say all of this, replacing 191's now-superseded
  "raising it further needs `MAX_POOL_SAMPLE` raised" note — keep that sentence's history, do not delete
  the reasoning trail.

The 40% reserve under the cap is deliberate: the audit's runtime is dominated by live third-party fetches
whose latency the loop does not control, so the budget must survive a slow day, not just today.

**2. Make the rotation leg clock-aware, so the raised ceiling can never cost a finished run.** Rotation
surfaces — and *only* rotation surfaces — become skippable under a wall-clock budget:
`AUDIT_TIME_BUDGET_MS`, defaulting to a value derived from the same 300s cap constant (never a second
literal — item-159 rule). Before rendering each rotation-picked surface, if elapsed run time already
exceeds the budget, skip that surface and every rotation surface after it.
- The anchor pool-detail block, the prescan-promoted surfaces, the static/text/i18n legs and every other
  named surface are **never** skippable. This guard exists to protect the run's completion, so it may
  only ever drop the leg that is explicitly best-effort.
- Truncation is **reported, never silent**: `poolRotation` gains the count actually rendered and a
  truncation flag, both surfaced in `signals/audit-findings.json` and in the console summary.

**3. The honesty trap — `seen` must record only what actually rendered.** `buildPoolSurfaces()` writes
the rotation state at *build* time (`audit-app.js:2353-2366`), and `runAudit()` persists it after the
render loop (`:3327`). If part 2 skips a surface, the persisted `seen` would claim coverage of a pool
that was never rendered — the coverage rate would inflate itself exactly where it is supposed to be
honest, and the error would compound across cycles. **The persisted `seen` must be reconciled to the ids
that actually rendered.** This is the single highest-risk line of the item and the first thing the
verifier should attack.

**Explicitly OUT of scope:** which pools are *selected* (183's seeded `sampleBySeed` rotation, 157/167's
prescan promotion, 154's same-seed-same-picks contract — all unchanged); parallel browser contexts or
browser reuse; `DEFAULT_POOL_PRESCAN_MAX`; the static-page leg's budget; any new signal, surface or
product change.

## Measurement
Metric: `poolRotation.seenCount` growth per tick and the scanner's total wall-clock, both read from each
heartbeat's `audit-findings.json`. · Window: 14 days (due **2026-08-14**, same window as 191 so the two
budget changes are read together) · Decision rule: **keep** if `seenCount` advances by the new sample
size each tick AND every run finishes inside the cap with the truncation flag false; **REVERT-CANDIDATE**
if truncation starts firing on ordinary ticks (the budget was set too high, or third-party latency moved)
or if any run exceeds the cap. Secondary read: whether a P0/P1 originates on a rotation-reached
pool-detail — informative if it happens, **uninformative if it does not**, at these per-pool daily odds.

## Territory notes
<!-- Build loop appends findings from the blindspot pass here. -->
- The rotation state is computed in `buildPoolSurfaces()` (`:2353-2366`) but persisted in `runAudit()`
  (`:3320-3333`), and persistence is opt-in via `opts.persistRotationState` (CLI only). The reconciliation
  in part 3 therefore belongs between those two points — in `runAudit()`, after the render loop, on the
  `poolResult.rotationState` object — not inside `buildPoolSurfaces()`, which has no way to know what
  rendered.
- The render loop (`:3279`) is serial over one `browser` instance with a fresh `newPage` per surface.
  Rotation-picked surfaces are the `extraSurfaces` built from `poolRotation.picked`; they are *not*
  currently distinguishable from prescan-promoted ones at render time (both are
  `pool-detail:<prefix>`, `kind: 'pool'`). The guard needs an explicit marker set at build time —
  reusing the name prefix as a proxy would also catch the promoted surfaces, which part 2 forbids
  skipping. **This is the most likely way to build the guard subtly wrong.**
- `seenCount` counts more than the rotation picks (191's own territory note): prescan-promoted ids and
  the anchor land in `seen` too. The full-pass line must stay derived from the rotation's rendered count
  against `candidateCount`, or the coverage claim drifts optimistic.
